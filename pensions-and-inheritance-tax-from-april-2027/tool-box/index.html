Inheritance tax on a pension from 6 April 2027: the tool box

By Steve Hunt ACII TEP · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026 · The law on this page was read at source on 19, 20 and 21 November 2026

What can be done about inheritance tax on a pension from 6 April 2027?

This page sets out ten headline tools for planning around inheritance tax on a pension from 6 April 2027: whole of life assurance in trust, a nominees' annuity, an impaired life annuity, gifts out of income, part of the home in trust, a purchased life annuity, a home income plan, business and agricultural property relief, outright gifts, and cover paid for by a business.

The ten lines below are the tool box from the one page factsheet for accountants. Under each line are the conditions and the law. Whether a tool fits depends on the client's facts.

The law behind the tax, with worked examples, is on its own page: Pensions and inheritance tax from 6 April 2027: what UK accountants need to know.

The tool box

The ten tools are headlines, as they appear on the one page factsheet.

  1. Whole of Life Assurance (WOL) ~ life cover that pays out on death, whenever death happens. Written in trust, the payout goes to the trust for the heirs and is normally outside the estate.

    The conditions. The policy is written in trust, so the payout goes to the trust for the heirs. Each premium is a gift. Premiums above the £3,000 annual exemption (section 19) rely on the exemption for normal expenditure out of income (section 21). Where an annuity has also been bought on the same life, the premiums count as normal expenditure only if the annuity and the policy are shown not to be associated operations (section 21(2)). A premium that no exemption covers is a lifetime gift into the trust.

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.19; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.21; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.21(2)
  2. Nominees' Annuity ~ an annuity bought with pension money in the member's lifetime that carries on being paid to a named person (the nominee) after death. The pension fund used leaves the estate, but buying one may count as a lifetime gift with a 7-year clock. Providers currently need the nominee to be 40 or over. Normal Expenditure out of Income (NEOI gifting surplus income) can also be used.

    The conditions. The annuity for the nominee is left out of the estate at death only where it was bought together with the member's own lifetime annuity (section 150A). Whether buying one counts as a lifetime gift is not settled: HMRC has not said and no court has ruled, so the 7-year point is Steve's analysis. The minimum age of 40 is the practice of the insurers that quoted. It is not a rule of law.

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.3A; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.7; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.21 · Provider evidence: Insurers' answers on the nominee's age, August 2026, held on file · Steve's analysis: Osborne's Get Out of Jail Card Under Attack (LinkedIn article)
  3. Impaired Life Annuity ~ also takes funds out of the estate immediately and again Normal Expenditure out of Income (NEOI gifting surplus income) can also be used. Either as gifts as cash or into a trust

    The conditions. A lifetime annuity with no guarantee period and no value protection stops at death, so no fund is left to count. A guarantee period or value protection leaves a payment that needs its own inheritance tax assessment. Gifts of the income are exempt only if they meet the three tests in line 4.

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.21 · HMRC: HMRC technical note: Inheritance Tax on pensions (updated 29 May 2026)
  4. Normal Expenditure out of Income (NEOI) ~ genuine regular gifts from surplus income are outside the estate immediately, with no 7-year clock.

    The conditions. Three tests, and all three must be met: the gift is part of the giver's normal expenditure, it is made out of income, and the giver is left with enough income to keep up the usual standard of living (section 21).

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.21
  5. Main property into trust ~ using an Interest in Possession Trust, part of the property value put into trust can be out of the estate after seven years, when set up correctly.

    The conditions. A lifetime gift into this kind of trust is not a potentially exempt transfer (section 3A). It is a chargeable transfer when it is made. Kept within the £325,000 nil rate band, with no other chargeable gifts in the seven years before, no inheritance tax is due on the way in, and after seven years the gift no longer counts (section 7). The trust is within the relevant property rules, with a charge at each ten-year anniversary and when property leaves the trust (sections 64 and 65). If the giver goes on living in the home, a full market rent must be paid for the share given away. If it is not, the gift with reservation rules treat that share as still in the estate (Finance Act 1986, sections 102 and 102B, and Schedule 20, paragraph 6).

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.3A; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.7; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.64; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.65; Finance Act 1986, s.102; Finance Act 1986, s.102B; Finance Act 1986, Schedule 20, paragraph 6
  6. Purchased Life Annuity ~ for those in good health where own funds are used to buy an annuity which again takes assets immediately out of the estate with no 7-year clock.

    The conditions. Buying an annuity from an insurer at the going rate is a purchase and not a gift, so the price paid is not a transfer of value (section 10). An annuity with no guarantee stops at death. The tax-free capital part of each payment is not income for the normal expenditure out of income exemption (section 21(3)), so gifts of that part need another exemption or come under the 7-year rule.

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.10; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.21(1), (3) and (4); Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.3A
  7. Home Income Plan ~ a loan secured on the main residence is used to buy an income for life. The loan is a debt, which can reduce the value of the estate.

    The conditions. A debt is taken into account in valuing the estate (section 5(3)), and a loan secured on the home is taken to reduce the value of the home (section 162(4)). At death the loan counts only so far as it is repaid out of the estate (section 175A). Why the estate value matters: above £2,000,000 an estate loses £1 of residence nil rate band for every £2 (section 8D). With 40% inheritance tax, that makes an effective 60% on the estate between £2,000,000 and £2,700,000, where two residence nil rate bands of £175,000 are available. How the loan and the annuity are treated depends on the plan.

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.5; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.162; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.175A; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.8D · HMRC: HMRC guidance: the residence nil rate band
  8. BPR and APR ~ using a business or agricultural property to secure assets up to £2.5 million where two years can apply and not seven.

    The conditions. The £2.5 million is one allowance for business property and agricultural property together (Finance Act 2026, Schedule 12, new section 124D). Qualifying property above the allowance gets 50% relief. The unused allowance of a husband, wife or civil partner who died first can be transferred (new section 124E). Business property must normally have been owned for two years (section 106). Agricultural property must have been occupied by the owner for two years, or owned for seven years and farmed throughout by the owner or someone else (section 117).

    Sources Law: Finance Act 2026, Schedule 12; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.106; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.117
  9. Pure gifting ~ gifts above the allowances carry a 7-year clock

    The conditions. A gift to a person is a potentially exempt transfer: no inheritance tax if the giver lives seven years. A gift into most trusts is a chargeable transfer when it is made (section 3A). The tax on a gift that fails tapers after three years (section 7).

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.19; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.3A; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.7
  10. Business owners ~ life cover can be arranged through the business: relevant life cover, key person cover and registered group life (death in service) cover. Registered group life benefits stay outside IHT after April 2027.

    The conditions. Qualifying death in service benefits are left out of the estate (section 150A). Key person cover pays the company, not the family.

    Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A · HMRC: HMRC technical note, 3.3.4 Death in service benefits
