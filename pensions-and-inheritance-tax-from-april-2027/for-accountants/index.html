Pensions and inheritance tax from 6 April 2027: what UK accountants need to know

By Steve Hunt ACII TEP · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026 · The law on this page was read at source on 19 and 20 November 2026

What changes for pensions and inheritance tax on 6 April 2027?

For deaths on or after 6 April 2027, most unused pension funds and pension death benefits count as part of the estate for UK inheritance tax (IHT), under the Finance Act 2026. A pension left to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity is normally still free of inheritance tax.

The pension scheme pays only the pension's own share of the estate's inheritance tax bill, and only when it is given a payment notice. The rest of the bill falls on the non-pension assets, which can mean the family home.

Add the income tax on what the family then draws and, in a worst case, the total tax can be more than the pension was worth.

Key facts: pensions and inheritance tax from 6 April 2027

  • Start date. Deaths on or after 6 April 2027 (Finance Act 2026, section 71).
  • What counts. Most unused pension funds and pension death benefits, including money left in drawdown. The Act calls the value "notional pension property".
  • What is left out. Dependants' scheme pensions, dependants' or nominees' annuities bought together with the member's own lifetime annuity, and qualifying death in service benefits.
  • Still exempt. A pension left to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity.
  • The pension's share. The tax on an estate is shared between its parts in proportion to their values (Inheritance Tax Act 1984, section 265).
  • Who pays. The personal representatives are liable. The pension scheme pays its share direct to HMRC within 35 days of a payment notice for at least £1,000 (section 226B).
  • When. Inheritance tax is due by the end of the sixth month after the month of death. Interest runs after that.
  • How many estates. HMRC estimates that in 2027/28 about 10,500 estates will pay inheritance tax that would not have before, and about 38,500 will pay more.

Two of the most common examples

Both examples assume a married couple, the second death, the home left to the children and all allowances unused.

Example 1   Mr and Mrs £1m estate

Current estate EXCLUDING pension£1,000,000
Combined DC pensions Mr & Mrs£500,000
IHT Prior to April 2027£0
IHT post April 2027£200,000

THIS IS THE CRITICAL PART…

The pension does not pay / cover the £200,000

IT IS ONLY CHARGED ITS SHARE based on the whole estate.

The pension is 1/3 of the whole estate so is only charged 1/3 of the IHT bill: £66,667

The remaining £133,333 comes from the NON-PENSION ASSETS and if that is the house then the house may have to be sold.

Example 2   Mr and Mrs £2m estate

Current estate EXCLUDING pension£2,000,000
Combined DC pensions Mr & Mrs£700,000
IHT Prior to April 2027£400,000
IHT post April 2027£820,000

And the total loss of the Residence Nil Rate Band (RNRB)

THIS IS THE CRITICAL PART…

The pension does not pay / cover the £420,000 it is responsible for

IT IS ONLY CHARGED ITS SHARE based on the whole estate.

The pension is 25.93% of the whole estate so is only charged 25.93% of the IHT bill: £212,593

The remaining £607,407 comes from the NON-PENSION ASSETS. That is £207,407 more than the £400,000 before.

Example 1 in short. With a £1,000,000 estate and £500,000 of pensions, the inheritance tax on the second death rises from nil to £200,000 for a death on or after 6 April 2027. The pension scheme pays £66,667 of it. The other £133,333 falls on the non-pension assets.

Example 2 in short. With a £2,000,000 estate and £700,000 of pensions, the inheritance tax rises from £400,000 to £820,000. The pension scheme pays £212,593. The other £607,407 falls on the non-pension assets.

How can the tax be more than the pension was worth?

Worst case. If:

  1. The parent who dies last is 75 or over
  2. Daughter draws the pension as income, about £24,000 a year
  3. Daughter already earns £100,000, so every £1 drawn is taxed at 60%
  4. The estate is over £2 million, as in Example 2

This can result in MORE TAX than the pension was worth!

Example 2: £420,000 of extra IHT, plus about £292,000 of income tax over 20 years, is about £712,000 of tax on a £700,000 pension (no growth, today's tax rates).

The worst case in short. On the facts of Example 2, a £700,000 pension can cause about £712,000 of tax: £420,000 of extra inheritance tax, plus about £292,000 of income tax paid by the daughter on what she draws over 20 years.

How is the inheritance tax on a pension worked out?

The pension's value is added to the rest of the estate, the inheritance tax is worked out on the total, and the bill is then shared between the pension and the other assets in proportion to their values (section 265).

Before 6 April 2027 a pension with discretionary death benefits normally sits outside the estate, and the examples assume that. In Example 1 the £1,000,000 estate is covered by £1,000,000 of allowances, so there is no inheritance tax. For a death on or after 6 April 2027 the pension is added in.

Example 1: the workings
Estate excluding pension£1,000,000
Pensions£500,000
Whole estate from 6 April 2027£1,500,000
Less two nil rate bands2 × £325,000£650,000
Less two residence nil rate bands2 × £175,000£350,000
Taxable£500,000
Inheritance tax at 40%£200,000
The pension's share£500,000 ÷ £1,500,000 = 1/3 of the bill£66,667
Falls on the non-pension assetsthe other 2/3 of the bill£133,333

In Example 2 the pension does two things. It is taxed itself, and it takes the estate over £2,000,000, so the residence nil rate bands are lost as well. The extra £420,000 is £280,000 of tax on the pension itself (40% of £700,000) plus £140,000 from the lost residence nil rate bands (40% of £350,000).

Example 2: the workings
Estate excluding pension£2,000,000
Pensions£700,000
Whole estate from 6 April 2027£2,700,000
Less two nil rate bands2 × £325,000£650,000
Less residence nil rate bands£1 is lost for every £2 over £2,000,000, so the whole £350,000 is lost at £2,700,000£0
Taxable£2,050,000
Inheritance tax at 40%£820,000
Inheritance tax before April 2027£2,000,000 less £1,000,000 of allowances, at 40%£400,000
Extra inheritance tax caused by the pension£420,000
The pension's share£700,000 ÷ £2,700,000 = 7/27, or 25.93%, of the £820,000 bill£212,593
Falls on the non-pension assetsthe other 74.07% of the bill£607,407

The facts behind the two examples

  • The first of the couple to die left everything to the survivor, including the pension. No inheritance tax was paid then and no allowance was used. The estate of the first to die was under £2 million.
  • The survivor still holds both pensions at the second death. An inherited pension that is still in drawdown counts in the survivor's estate.
  • The home is worth at least £350,000 and goes to the children or grandchildren.
  • The unused allowances of the first to die are claimed on the second death. No gifts were made in the seven years before it.
  • The whole estate is taxable, so the tax is shared in proportion to value. Where part of the estate is exempt or gets a relief, for example a share left to a charity, the shares change.

Which pension death benefits are left out, and what is exempt?

The excluded categories are dependants' scheme pensions; trivial commutation lump sum death benefits replacing those pensions; dependants' or nominees' annuities bought together with the member's own lifetime annuity; and qualifying death in service benefits. The benefit must be payable only in one or more of those excluded forms. Having a choice of a non-excluded benefit can prevent the exclusion, even if an excluded form is chosen after death. Annuity guarantee payments and value protection need their own inheritance tax assessment.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A · HMRC: HMRC technical note, 3.3.1 Dependants' scheme pension; HMRC technical note, 3.3.2 Trivial commutation; HMRC technical note, 3.3.3 Joint life annuities; HMRC technical note, 3.3.4 Death in service benefits; HMRC technical note 2, Dependants' scheme pensions

A pension that goes to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity is normally free of inheritance tax (sections 18 and 23). Tax may then be due when the second of the couple dies, as in the two examples.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.18; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.23

Who pays the inheritance tax on a pension, and can the scheme pay the whole bill?

Who is liable. The personal representatives are liable for the inheritance tax on the pension. A person in whom the pension property is vested, such as a beneficiary who receives it, is liable too. The trustees of a registered pension scheme are not liable as trustees. A trustee who is also the scheme administrator can still be liable as scheme administrator. The scheme administrator is liable only where a benefit is paid in breach of a withholding notice, or where the administrator fails to act on a payment notice (Inheritance Tax Act 1984, section 210, as rewritten by the Finance Act 2026, section 67).

Can the pension scheme pay the whole inheritance tax bill? No. The tax on an estate is attributed to its parts in proportion to their values (section 265). In the two examples the whole estate is taxable, so the pension bears its share and no more.

The payment notice. The personal representatives, or a beneficiary, can give the scheme administrator a payment notice. The notice must be for at least £1,000. It cannot be for more than the tax, with interest, that the person giving the notice is liable for on the pension in that scheme. It also cannot be for more than the benefits the scheme has still to pay out. The scheme administrator then has 35 days to pay HMRC (section 226B).

Why the scheme cannot pay more. Inheritance tax paid under section 226B is an authorised payment (Finance Act 2004, section 164). There is no authorised way for the scheme to pay the tax on the house or the other assets direct to HMRC. A payment the law does not authorise is not void, but it carries an unauthorised payment charge (sections 160 and 208). So the scheme cannot be told to pay more than the pension's share, and it cannot choose to pay more as an authorised payment.

Where the rest of the money comes from. The family can still use the pension to help with the rest of the bill. They draw benefits from what is left in the pension and pay the tax themselves. Where the member died at 75 or over, what they draw is taxed as their income first.

The withholding notice. The personal representatives, or people who expect to be the personal representatives, can also give the scheme administrator a withholding notice. While the notice runs, the scheme may not pay any beneficiary more than 50% of their entitlement. The limit does not apply to an excluded benefit or an exempt benefit, for example a benefit going to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity (section 226A(6)). The notice ends when the tax and interest are paid, when it is withdrawn, or 15 months after the end of the month of death, whichever comes first (section 226A).

Instalments. Inheritance tax on a house can be paid by ten equal yearly instalments, with interest, if the person paying elects. If the house is sold, the unpaid tax falls due at once (section 227). HMRC's technical note rules out instalments for the pension itself.

The new pension sections (210, 226A and 226B) apply only to deaths on or after 6 April 2027, so no court has yet ruled on them.

When is the inheritance tax on a pension due? A timeline for the executor and the accountant

The pension scheme has 28 days from a request to give the personal representatives the value of the pension. The inheritance tax is due by the end of the sixth month after the month of death, with interest after that. The scheme has 35 days to pay its share once it is given a payment notice. A withholding notice lasts no longer than 15 months after the end of the month of death.

  • Within 28 days of a request. The scheme administrator gives the personal representatives the value of the pension. The value can be a provisional estimate. The actual value then follows within 14 days of being ascertained.
  • By the end of the sixth month after the month of death. The inheritance tax is due. Interest runs on tax paid after that.
  • Within 35 days of a payment notice. The scheme administrator pays the pension's share to HMRC.
  • Until 15 months after the end of the month of death, at the latest. A withholding notice can hold back all but 50% of a beneficiary's entitlement.
  • Over ten years. Tax on a house can be paid by ten equal yearly instalments, with interest. Instalments are not available for the pension itself.

The worst case: how is the £712,000 worked out?

The £712,000 is £420,000 of extra inheritance tax plus about £292,000 of income tax paid by the daughter on what she draws from the pension over 20 years.

The income tax: the workings
Pension at death£700,000
Less the pension's share of the inheritance taxpaid by the scheme direct to HMRC£212,593
Left in the pension for Daughter£487,407
Drawn over 20 tax years, each year£24,370
Income tax at 60%, each year£14,622
Income tax over 20 years£292,444

Why 60%? Between £100,000 and £125,140 of adjusted net income, the personal allowance falls by £1 for every £2 (Income Tax Act 2007, section 35). Each extra £1 is taxed at 40%, and also loses 50p of allowance, which costs another 20p. That is 60p in the £1. The rates are today's rates (2026/27) for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland has its own rates.

Where the £712,000 falls
Inheritance tax paid out of the pension£212,593
Extra inheritance tax that falls on the non-pension assets£207,407
Income tax paid by Daughter on what she draws£292,444
Total tax caused by the £700,000 pension£712,444

The £712,000 is not all taken from the pension: about £207,000 of it is extra inheritance tax on the other assets. The example shows how far the tax can go on these facts. Change the facts and the figure changes:

  • If all of Daughter's pension income is taxed at 20%, the income tax is about £97,000, against about £292,000.
  • If Daughter takes all £487,407 in one tax year on top of her £100,000, the income tax is about £223,000 and the total is about £643,000.
  • If the parent who dies last is under 75, Daughter normally pays no income tax on drawdown income. Lump sums have their own allowance.

What did the Budget of 28 October 2026 change?

The main change, pensions counting for inheritance tax, was made law by the Finance Act 2026. The Budget of 28 October 2026 announced four further changes. None of the four is law yet. All are due in Finance Bill 2026-27.

  • Prospective personal representatives. People who expect to be the personal representatives will be able to direct a pension scheme administrator to pay the inheritance tax due on the pension to HMRC, when certain conditions are met. From 6 April 2027.
  • Gifts to charity. Those administering the estate will be able to choose whether the pension counts in the general part of the estate or as a separate part, when working out the lower 36% rate of inheritance tax for an estate that leaves enough to charity. From 6 April 2027.
  • The two-year rule. Pension death benefits that would be free of income tax will no longer become taxable just because they are paid more than two years after the scheme administrator learned of the death. For payments made on or after 28 October 2026.
  • Lump sums over the allowance. Where the member died under 75 and a lump sum death benefit is more than the lump sum and death benefit allowance, the tax on the excess will be assessed and collected through a new stand-alone process, charged on the beneficiary at their marginal rate. The income tax relief for inheritance tax paid on the same money is kept. The change has retrospective effect for relevant lump sum death benefits paid on or after 6 April 2024. The inheritance tax parts apply to deaths on or after 6 April 2027.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, Sch. 1A · HMRC: HMRC: Budget 2026, overview of tax legislation and rates, 28 October 2026, paragraphs 1.17, 1.18 and 1.27; HMRC policy paper, 28 October 2026: tax treatment of pension and lump sum death benefits under the two-year rule

The law, with links

Each link opens the section on legislation.gov.uk. The sections added by the Finance Act 2026 apply to deaths on or after 6 April 2027.

  • Finance Act 2026, s.71: the change applies to deaths on or after 6 April 2027.

  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.226A: withholding notices: no more than 50% of a beneficiary's entitlement is paid out, until 15 months after the end of the month of death at the latest. Excluded and exempt benefits are outside the limit.
  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.226B: payment notices: at least £1,000, no more than the tax and interest the person giving the notice is liable for on the pension, and no more than the benefits still to be paid; paid within 35 days.
  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.227: tax on land can be paid by ten equal yearly instalments.
  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.8A: the unused nil rate band of a husband, wife or civil partner who died first can be claimed on the second death.
  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.8D: the residence nil rate band, and its taper above £2 million.
  • Finance Act 2004, s.160: the payments a registered pension scheme is authorised to make.
  • Finance Act 2004, s.164: the list of authorised payments, which includes inheritance tax paid under section 226B.
  • Finance Act 2004, s.208: the tax charge on an unauthorised payment.
  • Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579CZA: drawdown income paid to a beneficiary is free of income tax where the member died under 75 and the conditions are met.
  • Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.567B: relief so that inheritance tax borne on the pension can be taken off before income tax is charged on it, where its conditions are met.
  • Income Tax Act 2007, s.35: the personal allowance falls by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000.
  • SI 2026/818, the Registered Pension Schemes (Provision of Information) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026: the timetable for a pension scheme to give the personal representatives the value of the pension: within 28 days of a request. A provisional estimate is allowed, with the actual value within 14 days of being ascertained.
  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.226: inheritance tax is due six months after the end of the month of death.
  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.233: interest on inheritance tax paid late.
