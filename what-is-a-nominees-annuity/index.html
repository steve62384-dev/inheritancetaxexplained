What is a nominees' annuity? Inheritance tax from April 2027

By Steve Hunt ACII TEP · Published 18 November 2026 · Last reviewed 18 November 2026 · Video 15 min · Full transcript below

The short answer

A nominees' annuity is a pension income for life, paid by an insurance company to someone you choose after you die, such as a grown-up son or daughter. Some insurers call it a non-dependent annuity, also spelt non-dependant annuity.

In the UK, from 6 April 2027, a nominees' annuity bought together with your own lifetime annuity is left out of the new inheritance tax charge on pensions. Your son or daughter still pays income tax on it, unless you die before 75.

Questions this video answers

What is a nominees' annuity?

A nominees' annuity is an income for life that is paid to your nominee after you die. It is a pension death benefit, and it is paid by an insurance company. A nominee is a person you choose who is not your dependant, most often a grown-up son or daughter. The law that defines a nominees' annuity is the Finance Act 2004, Schedule 28, paragraph 27AA. It can be bought together with your own lifetime annuity, or after you die with pension money that is left over.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch. 28, para. 27AA; Finance Act 2004, Sch. 28, para. 15

Is a non-dependent annuity the same as a nominees' annuity?

Yes. A non-dependent annuity is the name some insurers use for a nominees' annuity. It is the same thing. In pension law, your dependants are your husband, wife or civil partner, a child under 23, or someone who depended on you for money (Finance Act 2004, Schedule 28, paragraph 15). A nominee is someone who is not on that list, so an annuity for a nominee is an annuity for a non-dependant.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch. 28, para. 15; Finance Act 2004, Sch. 28, para. 27AA

What changes for pensions and inheritance tax on 6 April 2027?

From 6 April 2027, most unused pension money and most pension death benefits count as part of your estate for inheritance tax. The Finance Act 2026, section 66, puts a new section 150A into the Inheritance Tax Act 1984, and section 71 applies the change to deaths on or after 6 April 2027. If your estate is big enough to pay inheritance tax, the rate on the taxable part is 40%. If you die aged 75 or over, your son or daughter also pays income tax on the pension money they receive.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2026, s.66; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Finance Act 2026, s.71; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, Sch. 1; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579A; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.567B; Finance Act 2026, s.70

Is a nominees' annuity free of inheritance tax from April 2027?

A nominees' annuity bought together with your own lifetime annuity is left out of the new pension inheritance tax charge when you die. A nominees' annuity bought any other way is not. Section 150A(6)(c) of the Inheritance Tax Act 1984 lists "a dependants' annuity, or a nominees' annuity, that was purchased together with a lifetime annuity payable to the member". A nominees' annuity bought after you die, with your leftover pension money, does not get this exclusion.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Finance Act 2026, s.66

What does bought together mean?

Bought together means the nominees' annuity is linked to your own lifetime annuity when you buy it. It cannot be added years later. The two can be one joint life annuity, or two separate annuities bought no more than seven days apart (Finance Act 2004, section 278B).

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.278B; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A

Does my son or daughter pay income tax on a nominees' annuity?

It depends on your age when you die. If you die aged 75 or over, your son or daughter pays income tax on each payment, at their own rate, as the payments arrive (Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, sections 579A and 579D). If you die before 75, the payments from a nominees' annuity bought together with your own annuity can be free of income tax (section 646B(3)). There are conditions.

Sources Law: Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579A; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579D; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.646B

What do the numbers look like?

In Steve's made-up example, Mr Miggins is 80 and dies after 6 April 2027 with £100,000 still in his pension pot. Inheritance tax at 40% takes £40,000 and leaves £60,000. His daughter Amy then pays 40% income tax on the £60,000, which is £24,000, so she is left with about £36,000. If Mr Miggins had bought a nominees' annuity for Amy together with his own lifetime annuity, there would be no pension pot left to tax, and Amy would pay income tax on each payment as it arrives.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, Sch. 1; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579A · Steve's analysis: The Mr Miggins worked example in video 7: arithmetic on the stated assumptions

What are the catches?

There are six. The nominees' annuity has to be bought together with your own lifetime annuity. Once the cooling off period has passed, an annuity usually cannot be changed, cancelled or cashed in. Your own income is lower. If your son or daughter dies before you, the nominee's part usually just stops, depending on the contract. Not every annuity provider offers a nominees' annuity, and some insurers set a minimum age for the nominee. And there is an open legal question about gifts.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.278B · Provider evidence: Insurers' answers on the nominee's age, August 2026, held on file · Steve's experience: Steve Hunt, in UK financial services since 1980

Is buying a nominees' annuity a gift for inheritance tax?

There is no definitive legal answer yet. Section 150A deals with what happens when you die. It does not settle whether buying the nominees' annuity in the first place was a gift, which the Inheritance Tax Act 1984 calls a transfer of value (section 3(1)). Sections 10 and 12(2ZA) also matter, and the Supreme Court case HMRC v Parry [2020] UKSC 35 did not decide the point. If buying one were treated as a gift, the usual seven year rule for gifts would apply (section 3A).

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.3; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.10; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.12; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.3A · Case law: HMRC v Parry and others [2020] UKSC 35 (Supreme Court)

What are the three questions to keep apart?

One: is the nominees' annuity counted for inheritance tax when you die? For one bought together with your own lifetime annuity, the law says no, from 6 April 2027. Two: does your son or daughter pay income tax on it? Yes if you die at 75 or over, and it can be tax free if you die before 75. Three: was buying it a gift? The law has not settled that yet.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579A; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.646B; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.3

What does bought together mean? Bought together means the nominees' annuity is linked to your own lifetime annuity when you buy it. It cannot be added years later. That is the Finance Act 2004, section 278B.

So the nominees' annuity has to be arranged at the start, when you buy your own annuity.

Does my son or daughter pay income tax on a nominees' annuity? It depends on your age when you die. If you die aged 75 or over, your son or daughter pays income tax on each payment, at their own rate, as the payments arrive. If you die before 75, the payments can be free of income tax.

For deaths at 75 or over, the rules are in the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, sections 579A and 579D. The tax is paid year by year, spread over the rest of their life. It is not all taxed in one go. For deaths before 75, the rule for a nominees' annuity bought together with your own annuity is section 646B, subsection 3, of the same Act.

There are conditions, but the income can be tax free. So being left out of inheritance tax does not always mean free of income tax. They are two different taxes, with two different sets of rules.

What are the catches? There are six catches to know about. First, timing. The nominees' annuity has to be bought together with your own lifetime annuity. You cannot add one later. 11:20Is buying a nominees' annuity a gift for inheritance tax?

Is buying a nominees' annuity a gift for inheritance tax? There is no definitive legal answer yet. The exclusion in section 150A deals with what happens when you die. It does not settle whether buying the nominees' annuity in the first place was a gift. Under the Inheritance Tax Act 1984, section 3, subsection 1, a gift for inheritance tax is called a transfer of value. It is anything that makes your estate worth less.

Two other sections matter. Section 10 says a deal is not a transfer of value if you did not mean to give anyone a free benefit, and the deal was at arm's length. Section 12, subsection 2ZA, protects pension rights that are not used. In 2020 the Supreme Court decided a pension inheritance tax case, HMRC v Parry. That case was about moving a pension and not taking benefits.

It did not decide whether buying a nominees' annuity is a gift. If buying one were treated as a gift to your son or daughter, the usual seven year rule for gifts would apply. That is section 3A of the Inheritance Tax Act 1984. Some families cover that seven year risk with life cover written in trust, such as a whole of life plan.
