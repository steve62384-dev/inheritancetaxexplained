How much of my pension will be taxed when I die? A 103% UK example

By Steve Hunt ACII TEP · Published 3 November 2026 · Last reviewed 3 November 2026 · Video 17 min · Full transcript below

The short answer

How much of your pension is taxed when you die depends on two things: when you die, and how old you are. From 6 April 2027, in the UK, most pensions you have not spent count as part of your estate for inheritance tax. If you die at 75 or over, your family also pays income tax on what they draw out.

In Steve's made-up example, with real numbers for the 2026/27 tax year, a £500,000 pension ends up costing one family £516,000 in tax: 103% of the pension. One name on the expression of wish form, added before 6 April 2027, could have made it nought.

Key facts (as at 2 November 2026)

  • From 6 April 2027, most unused pension funds and pension death benefits count as part of the estate for inheritance tax. The Finance Act 2026 added section 150A to the Inheritance Tax Act 1984, which calls them notional pension property. Death in service lump sums and some pensions paid to dependants are excluded.Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Finance Act 2026, s.66; Finance Act 2026, s.71 · HMRC: HMRC technical note: Inheritance Tax on pensions (updated 29 May 2026)
  • A pension that goes to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity is normally free of inheritance tax (sections 18 and 23). Tax may then be due when the second of the couple dies.Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.18; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.23
  • The pension pays its share of the whole estate's tax bill, in proportion to its value (section 265). Steve calls this the sharing rule. The executors, or the family, can ask the provider to pay the pension's share with a payment notice under section 226B: within 35 days, and only if the share is £1,000 or more.Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.265; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.226B · HMRC: HMRC technical note 2, 7.6 Amount withheld
  • If the person dies at 75 or over, the family pays income tax at their own rate on what they draw (Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, section 579CZA covers the under 75 exemption). The inheritance tax paid out of the pension is not taxed again as income (section 567B).Sources Law: Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579CZA; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.567B · HMRC: HMRC Pensions Tax Manual, PTM072430 (beneficiaries' drawdown and income tax)
  • HMRC estimates that in 2027/28 about 10,500 estates will pay inheritance tax that would not have before, and about 38,500 will pay more: 49,000 estates in the first year.Sources HMRC: HMRC policy paper: Inheritance Tax on unused pension funds and death benefits, 26 November 2025
  • While Mum is alive, the trustees can only pay a grown-up child a pension in their own name if the child is named on the form (Finance Act 2004, Schedule 28, paragraph 27A). Changing a form that does not bind the trustees is not a gift for inheritance tax (IHTM17052 and IHTM17071).Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch. 28, para. 27A · HMRC: HMRC Inheritance Tax Manual, IHTM17052; HMRC Inheritance Tax Manual, IHTM17071

The Miggins family are made up, but their numbers are real. Mr Miggins is 70, seriously ill, with a £500,000 personal pension he has never touched. He dies in January 2027 aged 71; his form says "Wife". Mrs Miggins leaves the money in the pension and dies in 2029 aged 76. Her other estate is £2 million, most of it the house, which goes to their daughter Amy. She has her own and her husband's full nil rate bands and residence nil rate bands. Amy is 45, lives in England, earns £100,000 and draws £24,000 a year for 15 years. The tax figures use UK rules and bands for 2026/27, with no growth. Scottish income tax rates are different.

Questions this video answers

How much of my pension will be taxed when I die?

It depends on when you die and how old you are. From 6 April 2027 most unused UK pensions count for inheritance tax, and if you die at 75 or over your family also pays income tax on what they draw. In Steve's made-up example, with real 2026/27 numbers, a £500,000 pension costs one family £516,000 in tax, or 103%.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Finance Act 2026, s.71; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579CZA · Steve's analysis: The Miggins worked example in video 6: arithmetic on the stated assumptions

Will my pension count for inheritance tax when I die?

Yes, if you die on or after 6 April 2027. Most pensions you have not spent count in your estate, including money in drawdown you have not taken out. Before that date most pensions where the trustees decide who is paid sit outside your estate. This is about personal and workplace pension pots; the State Pension works differently. A pension that goes to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity is normally free of inheritance tax.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Finance Act 2026, s.66; Finance Act 2026, s.71; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.18; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.23 · HMRC: HMRC technical note: Inheritance Tax on pensions (updated 29 May 2026)

How much inheritance tax will my pension pay?

Your pension pays its share of the whole estate's tax bill, in proportion to its value, under section 265 of the Inheritance Tax Act 1984. If your pension is one fifth of your estate, it pays one fifth of the bill. HMRC's Technical Note 2 (27 August 2026) says the same. The provider pays the pension's share within 35 days of a payment notice, if it is £1,000 or more.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.265; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.226B · HMRC: HMRC technical note 2, 7.6 Amount withheld

Will my family pay income tax on my pension as well?

Yes, if you die at 75 or over. Money drawn from an inherited pension is tax free if the person it came from died under 75; if they died at 75 or over, every pound drawn counts as the family member's own taxable income. At the Budget on 28 October 2026 the government said it is removing the old two-year time limit for payments from that date; the law is still to come.

Sources Law: Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579CZA; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.567B · HMRC: HMRC Pensions Tax Manual, PTM072430 (beneficiaries' drawdown and income tax) · Published source: HM Treasury: Budget 2026, Overview of Tax Legislation and Rates, 28 October 2026, paragraph 1.22 (High Value Council Tax Surcharge, Finance Bill 2026-27, from April 2028)

How can the tax on a pension come to more than 100%?

In Steve's example Mr Miggins dies in January 2027 at 71, with "Wife" on his form, so there is no tax then. Mrs Miggins leaves the money in the pension and dies in 2029 at 76. Now her inherited pension counts in her estate, which HMRC's Technical Note 2 confirms, and because she died over 75 their daughter Amy pays income tax on every pound she draws.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003, s.579CZA · HMRC: HMRC technical note 2, Death of a beneficiary (example 8) · Steve's analysis: The Miggins worked example in video 6: arithmetic on the stated assumptions

What does the Miggins family actually pay?

£516,000 on a £500,000 pension: £300,000 of extra inheritance tax and £216,000 of income tax. With the pension her estate is £2.5 million, so £250,000 of residence nil rate band is lost (section 8D) and the tax rises from £400,000 to £700,000. The pension pays one fifth, £140,000, leaving £360,000 for Amy. Drawing £24,000 a year on top of £100,000, she pays an effective 60% (Income Tax Act 2007, section 35): £216,000.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.8D; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.265; Income Tax Act 2007, s.35 · HMRC: GOV.UK: Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances · Steve's analysis: The Miggins worked example in video 6: arithmetic on the stated assumptions

Why is it 103% and not the 67% in the papers?

Most headlines take 40% inheritance tax off the pension, then 45% income tax off what is left: 67%. Here three things work together: the £2 million taper, Amy's 60% rate and the sharing rule. The pension added £300,000 to the bill but paid only £140,000, so £160,000 more was left in it, and Amy pays 60% on that too. If the pension had paid the full £300,000, the total would be £420,000, or 84%.

Sources Law: Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.8D; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.265; Income Tax Act 2007, s.35 · Steve's analysis: The Miggins worked example in video 6: arithmetic on the stated assumptions

Can one name on my expression of wish form change this?

Yes, if you die before 6 April 2027 and under 75. While your wife is alive, the trustees can only pay a grown-up child a pension in their own name if you named them on the form. With "Amy, daughter, 10%" added, the trustees could pay Amy directly: no inheritance tax, no income tax, and nothing in Mum's estate. Changing a form that does not bind the trustees is not a gift for inheritance tax.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch. 28, para. 27A · HMRC: HMRC Inheritance Tax Manual, IHTM17052; HMRC Inheritance Tax Manual, IHTM17071 · Steve's analysis: The Miggins worked example in video 6: arithmetic on the stated assumptions

What's the catch?

There are six. The nought needs death before 6 April 2027 and under 75. The trustees decide. Not every pension offers a pension to a named child. If the children are paid, the husband or wife may get less. Whatever Amy still has in the pension when she dies counts in her own estate. And the 103% depends on the numbers; Scottish income tax rates are different.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch. 28, para. 27A; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A; Finance Act 2026, s.71 · Steve's analysis: The Miggins worked example in video 6: arithmetic on the stated assumptions

What can I check today?

Check who is named on your expression of wish form for every pension you have. Ask your provider for a copy, ask whether they can pay your children a pension in their own name, not just a lump sum, and follow your provider's form.

How much inheritance tax will my pension pay? Your pension pays its share of the whole estate's tax bill, in proportion to its value. Section 265 of the Inheritance Tax Act 1984 shares the tax out between everything in the estate, by value. I call this the sharing rule. 7:22What does the Miggins family actually pay?

What does the Miggins family actually pay? The Miggins family pays £516,000 in tax on a £500,000 pension: £300,000 of extra inheritance tax and £216,000 of income tax. Mrs Miggins' other estate is £2 million, most of it the house. The house goes to Amy, and Amy lives in England, not Scotland. Mrs Miggins has her own allowances and her husband's.

That is £650,000 of nil rate band, and £350,000 of residence nil rate band, the extra allowance for passing a home to children. £1 million in all. Without the pension, the inheritance tax is 40% of £1 million. That is £400,000. With the pension, her estate is £2.5 million. Above £2 million, the residence nil rate band is cut by £1 for every £2, under section 8D. She loses £250,000 of it.

Her allowances fall to £750,000. The tax is 40% of £1.75 million. That is £700,000. So the pension has added £300,000 to the bill. But under the sharing rule, the pension is one fifth of the estate, so it pays one fifth of the £700,000. That is £140,000. The rest of the estate, the house money, pays the rest. Its bill goes up from £400,000 to £560,000. That leaves £360,000 in the pension for Amy.

Amy earns £100,000. She draws £24,000 a year for 15 years, which takes her income to £124,000 each year. Between £100,000 and £125,140, she loses £1 of her personal allowance for every £2 of extra income, under section 35 of the Income Tax Act 2007. So every pound she draws is taxed at an effective 60%. 60% of £360,000 is £216,000. Add it up. £300,000 of extra inheritance tax, plus £216,000 of income tax.

That is £516,000. On a £500,000 pension, that is 103%. The family hands over every pound Dad saved, and threepence on top. Put another way, the family ends up £16,000 worse off than if Dad had never saved a penny into the pension. The 103% compares the family's extra tax with the size of the pension. No single tax is charged at 103%.
